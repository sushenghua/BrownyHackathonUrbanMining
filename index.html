Western Corridor Urban Mining

A Council-facing tool that shows how much building material could be recovered from houses along Auckland's Western Rail Corridor, and what it's worth, before those houses become waste.

Challenge: Waste · Construction & demolition materials Built in one day Team of 6

Demo walkthrough (1:36): filtering by area, zooming to the house level, and browsing materials and price sources.

The problem

Hidden value

Auckland's houses hold large amounts of reusable timber, roofing, bricks, metals, doors and windows. But nobody can see how much exists or where it is, so most of it ends up as demolition waste.

Wrong scale

Recovery is usually judged house by house. As the city plans to intensify around rail stations, Council and industry need a neighbourhood- and corridor-level view to make the case for deconstruction over demolition.

  • There was no existing tool that looks at this.
  • Other cities (Amsterdam, Vienna, Singapore) already map their "urban mines" to forecast future waste.

Our solution

Materials Explorer

An interactive map of the corridor. Pick a station catchment, local board or ward and see expected recoverable materials by type, split into what can be reused and what can be recycled, with an indicative market value. Zoom in to inspect any single house.

Open API

Every number in the interface is also available as JSON or GeoJSON, so Council and recovery operators can use the results in their own tools.

How it works

Map the buildings

Start from public building footprints and keep low-rise houses in the corridor.

Estimate materials

Apply recovery rates from real Auckland deconstruction projects, per square metre of floor area.

Group by area

Roll houses up into station catchments, local boards and wards, without double counting.

Put a value on it

Multiply tonnes by editable, sourced market prices, with stale prices flagged in the app.

Who it's for

Council waste & planning teams

Size the opportunity and plan for future recovery.

Waste & recovery operators

Understand the market for recovered materials, neighbourhood by neighbourhood.

Designers & builders

See what salvaged materials could become available nearby.

This is a one-day proof of concept: figures assume every house is deconstructed (it is not a demolition forecast), and prices are placeholders.
